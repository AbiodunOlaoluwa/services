Privacy notice

How I handle the details of people I contact about my work, and what you can ask me to do with them.

Who I am

I'm Olaoluwa Abiodun, a software engineer working as a sole trader from Lagos, Nigeria. I decide how the personal details described here are used, which makes me the controller under UK and EU data protection law (UK GDPR and EU GDPR), and Nigeria's Data Protection Act 2023 also applies to me. You can reach me about anything on this page at mate.olaoluwa@gmail.com.

Whose details this covers

  • People I email about my services: usually owners, directors and managers at letting agencies, recruitment agencies, and automation or software agencies.
  • People who email me or become clients.
  • People who visit these pages.

When I build something for a client, the data inside their systems (for example their applicants' or candidates' details) is covered by my agreement with that client and by the client's own privacy notice, not this one.

What I collect

Your name, job title, work email address, the company you work for, a work phone number if it's published, and public information about your company: its website, job adverts, and its record at Companies House or a similar register. I also keep the emails we exchange and short notes on what we discussed.

Where it comes from

Your company's own website, public job adverts, Companies House, and public business directories such as partner and expert listings. When a work email address isn't published, I sometimes work it out from the company's usual email format, or look it up with an email-finding service. I then check it with an email-verification service before I write. If you email me first, it comes from you.

Why I use it, and on what basis

To contact you about work I could do for your business, to follow up a small number of times if you don't reply, and to stay in touch if you've told me you'd like that. The lawful basis is legitimate interests: telling businesses about relevant services, with a short, limited sequence of emails (at most three per approach, unless you reply). If you become a client, I use your details to deliver the work, under our contract.

Tools that handle your details

  • Google (Gmail, Google Sheets and Google Drive), where I send email and keep my contact list and notes.
  • Notion, where I keep a short list of people who asked me to stay in touch.
  • An email-verification service (Bouncer) and, occasionally, an email-finding service, which check or find a work address.
  • AI tools, namely Anthropic's Claude and OpenAI's ChatGPT, which help me research companies and draft and review my emails. Your name, role, company and work email can appear in that text.
  • GitHub, which hosts these pages and may record technical details such as IP addresses when you visit, for security. These pages don't use cookies, analytics or tracking.

Where your details are handled

I work from Nigeria, and most of the tools above are run by companies based in the United States. So your details are handled outside the UK and the EU. Where those providers move personal data out of the UK or EU, they rely on recognised safeguards such as standard contractual clauses.

How long I keep it

Until 12 months after we were last in touch, unless you become a client (then for as long as the work and the law require) or you ask me to stop sooner. If you ask me not to contact you again, I keep only your email address on a do-not-contact list, so that I don't write to you by mistake.

Your rights

You can ask for a copy of the details I hold about you, ask me to correct or delete them, or ask me to limit how I use them. You can object at any time to being contacted, and I'll stop. The simplest way is to reply "stop" to any email from me, or write to mate.olaoluwa@gmail.com. I'll reply within one month.

If you're not happy with how I've handled your details, you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office in the UK (ico.org.uk), to the data protection authority in your EU country, or to the Nigeria Data Protection Commission (ndpc.gov.ng). I'd appreciate the chance to put it right first.

Changes

If I change how I use personal details, I'll update this page and the date at the top.
