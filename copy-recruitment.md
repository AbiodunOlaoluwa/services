## Where I'm coming from
Most of my recruiting has been sourcing for early-stage US startups: I've contacted about 1,000 candidates through personalised four-step email sequences, contributed to five placements, and contributed to the workflows that found candidates' contact details and drafted each email's opening line. Finding people who fit, finding an email that works, and writing a first line that doesn't read like a template are the parts I build for agencies.

## What I build
**Sourcing for a live role.** You send the brief and choose the sources. For engineering or construction that might be company team pages, project announcements, public professional registers and conference speaker lists. You set the criteria (must-haves, nice-to-haves, location), and the list shows which ones each person meets and where that came from, so your consultant can check it and change the criteria.

**Checked work emails.** The address is worked out from the company's own email pattern, then verified with an email-checking service, which confirms the mail server accepts that address. It can't promise the person reads it. If no address verifies, the person is listed without one rather than guessed. On an outreach list I built for my own project, this took bounces from 12.3% to 2.9%.

**One record per person.** The same person found on two sites becomes one record, so nobody gets two messages from you.

**A draft first line for each person,** based on something they actually did, like a project they delivered or a talk they gave. Your consultant approves, edits or bins it. Nothing sends on its own.
